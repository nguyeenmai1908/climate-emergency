Before You Arrive
~5 minutes

You've read. You've watched. You've spent time with ideas that are designed to unsettle comfortable assumptions.

One last thing before class: sit with a question. You don't need to write a polished response — just let it stay with you on the walk home, or over dinner, or first thing in the morning.

Before you write — take a moment to think
Consider what has stayed with you from the reading and video:
  • Was there a moment — an image, an argument, a sentence — that stopped you? What was it?
  • Where did you feel resistance, discomfort, or disagreement? What does that reaction tell you?
  • What do you already carry into this course — from your own life, your family, your community?
Reflection Prompt
"What is your earliest memory of sensing that something was wrong with the world — ecologically, socially, or both? And what did you do with that feeling?"

Take time to sit with this question — jot notes, think it through, let it stay with you. It will seed your first in-class conversation and connects directly to Assignment 1: My Story with Climate.

What you've engaged with this week
01 Krenak's provocation: Earth and humanity are not separate entities. Our alienation from the organism we belong to is at the root of the crisis — and at the root of how we teach it.
02 The Humanity Club: The idea of a singular, universal humanity has historically excluded those who relate to the world differently — and climate change falls hardest on those left outside.
03 Two lenses: Krenak's relational philosophy and the Superorganism's systems framing are not the same — but they are both trying to see the same thing. Your classroom needs both.
04 Your story matters: Climate education begins with the educator's own relationship to the emergency. Knowing your story — where you came from, what you carry — is not optional. It is the work.
