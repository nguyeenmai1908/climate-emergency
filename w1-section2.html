Meet Ailton Krenak
~5 minutes

Before you open the reading, a little context — not to tell you what to think, but to help you orient yourself as you enter the text.

Ideas to Postpone the End of the World is not a policy document or a science report. It is a philosophical provocation — three lectures, delivered in Lisbon, by an Indigenous thinker whose life and land have been shaped by exactly the forces this course examines.

About the Author
Ailton Krenak

Ailton Krenak is a Brazilian Indigenous leader, philosopher, and environmental activist from the Krenak Nation. His homeland runs along the Doce River — called Watu, meaning "grandfather" — in Minas Gerais, Brazil.

In 2015, a mining disaster sent 43.7 million cubic metres of toxic mud into Watu, putting the river into what Krenak calls "a coma." This loss shapes everything in the text you are about to read.

Krenak was instrumental in writing Indigenous land rights into Brazil's 1988 Constitution. He remains one of the most important living voices on colonialism, ecological destruction, and what it means to be human on this Earth.

As you read, notice
Four questions to hold while you're in the text
  1. How does Krenak describe the relationship between humanity and Earth? What assumptions does he challenge — and what does he put in their place?
  2. What does he mean by the "Humanity Club" — and who gets left out of it? How does this connect to the colonial histories you may already know?
  3. What do concepts like Watu as grandfather or the Takukrak mountains tell us about different ways of knowing and relating to place?
  4. What does any of this have to do with your role as an educator — in this moment, in this country, with these students?
