Welcome & Framing
~3 minutes

Climate change education is not only about data, science, or policy.

It is also about how we live within the cycles of life — how we feel and sense our entanglement with the Earth's metabolism, and how our exchanges with one another and with the world around us shape the future we share.

This week we begin at the beginning. Not with facts or frameworks, but with a question: What does the climate emergency actually mean for an educator? Not just what to teach — but how to show up to it. With your students. With your community. With yourself.

Your pre-class work
  • 01 Read this short orientation to Ailton Krenak and his ideas — Section 2 ~5 minutes
  • 02 Read Ideas to Postpone the End of the World — the PDF is attached in Section 2 ~60–90 minutes
  • 03 Watch The Superorganism and the Future — embedded in Section 3 ~45 minutes
  • 04 Sit with one reflection question in Section 4 before you arrive No formal submission

The module itself takes about 15 minutes. The reading and video are the real work — give them time and space, not a cramped half-hour between other tasks.

Come to class having sat with Krenak's ideas. There are no right answers to bring. There are only the questions that stayed with you.

"My main reason for postponing the end of the world is so we've always got time for one more story. If we can make time for that, then we'll be forever putting off the world's demise." — Ailton Krenak
